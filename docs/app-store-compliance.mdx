This page covers what a published Restage surface can and can't change, and how that
relates to the store rules that usually come up. It isn't legal advice and it doesn't
guarantee approval. You're still responsible for your app and how it uses remote content.

## What a published surface contains

A Restage surface uses the [Remote Flutter Widgets](https://pub.dev/packages/rfw) format.
What ships is a description that your app's widgets draw. It's written in a small closed
language that the compiler checks. It can carry simple logic, like conditions over data
your app supplies.

Gestures, drawing, network calls and native work stay in Dart compiled into your app. New
kinds of behavior still ship as an app release.

An update can change:

- Copy, layout and style values.
- Flow routing.
- Conditions and loops over data your app supplies.
- Which registered event or action a control uses.
- Where the parts of a screen that render from your app go, and what's in their slots.

Widget implementations, gesture handlers, network clients and native operations all come
from your app build. The parts of a screen the build couldn't deliver also render from
your app's compiled code, and your own callbacks run there. A published version never
carries that code. The boundary on this page stays the same.

## Apple App Review

[Apple's App Review Guideline 2.5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#software-requirements)
covers downloading, installing or executing code that introduces or changes application
features or functionality. A Restage surface carries no Dart and no script. It's a
description in a closed language the compiler checks. Widgets and typed actions compiled
into the app draw it.

[Guideline 4.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#mini-apps-mini-games-streaming-games-chatbots-plug-ins-and-game-emulators)
covers apps that offer certain forms of software not embedded in the binary, including
HTML5 mini apps and mini games. In Restage, native platform access lives in your own
widgets and registered host actions. Check whether your app's use of remote content falls
under any part of that guideline.

## Hidden and dormant features

[Guideline 2.5.19](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#software-requirements)
asks that an app contain no hidden, dormant or undocumented features. Its functionality
should be clear to users and to App Review.
[Guideline 2.3.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata)
asks that new features be described with specificity in the Notes for Review and be
accessible for review. Until October 16, 2026 both sentences lived in 2.3.1. Older
rejections cite that number.

These are the guidelines most likely to come up for an app that changes screens over the
air, whichever tool delivers them. Two things about Restage help:

- The surface you wrote is compiled into the build. A reviewer reaches it like any other
  screen.
- A published update arranges widgets and actions already in that reviewed build. New
  behavior ships as an app release.

The rest is yours to do:

- **Declare it.** Name Restage in the Notes for Review. List the screens it delivers and
  say what a delivered update can change. A starting point:

  ```text
  This app uses Restage (restage.dev) to deliver layout and copy updates to these
  screens: <screens>. Each screen is compiled into this build and reachable at <path>
  with the demo account below. A delivered update is data rendered by widgets and
  actions already in this binary. Code, features, products and native capabilities ship
  in an app update. Which version a device receives depends on <the active version |
  a random split among the versions listed | rules on country, language or app build>.
  The review device is assigned like any other.
  ```

- **Keep every surface reachable.** Give the reviewer a route to each delivered screen.
  That means a demo account, no sign-up wall in front of it, and no targeting rule that
  excludes the reviewer's device or region. The review device is a fresh install on
  a new device. A rule on country, platform, device class or app build that such an
  install can't match keeps the version from the reviewer too.
- **Keep review state out of host data.** Your app can pass values into a surface as host
  data. Say the app computes a value to recognize a review device, from a sandbox
  receipt, TestFlight, an IP range or a remote "review mode" flag. If it passes that value
  in so the surface shows something different, that's review-environment detection. It doesn't matter what delivers the flag. Third-party starter kits sometimes ship such a switch, and you should remove it before you submit.
- **Mind the names.** App Review reads the binary and what the app fetches during review.
  A name is enough to raise a flag, even when the code that carries it is unreachable on
  that platform. Flutter compiles every platform's branches into every build. An
  Android-only switch is present in the iOS binary. A workaround built for one store's
  review also ships to the other store's reviewers in the same build. Don't name a
  surface, version, audience, event, host data key or Dart symbol after review, reviewers
  or bypassing. Renaming or obfuscating such a switch makes it worse. Guideline 2.5.19
  names hiding features in the binary "through code obfuscation or other methods" as
  grounds for removal and termination of the developer account.
- **Stay inside what was reviewed.** Copy, layout, ordering, styling and the choice among
  products and actions the reviewed build already offers are updates. A delivered surface
  that introduces a feature, a product or a claim the reviewed build didn't have is a new
  feature under this guideline. It belongs in a release with its own review notes.
- **Serve App Review what your users get.** Audience routing and experiments choose among
  published versions for your users. Using them to show a reviewer something different
  from what users receive can end a developer account. The guidelines' Introduction names
  trying to trick the review process as grounds for removal. Guideline 2.3.1(b) makes
  repeated behavior grounds for removal from the Apple Developer Program.
- **Keep subscription disclosures in every version.** A published paywall keeps the
  disclosures [Guideline 3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#subscriptions)
  requires in every version: the price, the billing period, what renews, and how to
  cancel. A version that drops them breaks the rule even when reviewers and users see the
  same screen.
- **Treat reserved widgets as building blocks.** It's ordinary to carry catalog widgets
  your app hasn't drawn yet, or a [reserve screen](/delivery/app-size). Buttons, sliders,
  cards and layout are parts, and every design system ships parts it doesn't use on every
  screen. A widget that is itself a feature, such as a chat panel, a camera capture or a
  checkout, should be visible in the reviewed build or described in the notes before any
  delivered surface uses it.

### If App Review cites 2.5.19 or 2.3.1

App Review uses the phrase "review-environment detection" for code or configuration that
behaves differently when the app is under review. Rejections before October 16, 2026 cite
2.3.1(a) for it. Newer ones cite 2.5.19, with 2.3.1(a) for the Notes for Review.

Check these in order:

- Any condition on sandbox receipts or TestFlight.
- Routing rules a fresh install can't match.
- Host data passed into a surface that depends on such a condition.
- Third-party kits with a review-mode switch.
- Your Notes for Review.

In Resolution Center, name Restage, list the delivered screens and say how versions are
assigned. The dashboard keeps the version history and the routing rules for each surface.
Offer them as the record of what was served to which audience.

## Google Play

[Google Play's device and network abuse policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16559646)
restricts applications that download executable code outside Google Play, such as dex,
JAR and native library files. The Restage SDK reads surface data and renders it with
widgets compiled into the app.

For purchases during testing, add the accounts that will test your app as
[license testers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test) in Play
Console. Their purchases complete with test payment methods and are never charged. Test
subscriptions renew on an accelerated schedule.

When your app gates content behind a subscription or membership, Google asks for access
details in the
[App access](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455)
section of Play Console. You give it a test account and instructions, with up to five
sets. Give the reviewer an account that already holds the subscription, through a license
tester purchase or an entitlement you grant on the server. Don't add a way past the
paywall in the app itself. A bypass key or flag is what review systems look for, and it
reaches Apple's reviewers in the same build.

## Interaction stays in your app

Interactive catalog widgets can contain gesture, animation or navigation logic, and that code ships with the app. The surface sets the widget's properties and receives its events.

Host actions work the same way. Your app registers a named, versioned action with typed
arguments and results, and a flow can call it. The handler is Dart code compiled into the
app. Adding or changing one needs an app release.

## Compatibility and fallback

Source the compiler can't express stays in your app binary and renders from there. A
published version never carries it. On the device, the SDK refuses a published version
that doesn't match the widgets, custom widgets, app-rendered parts or flow declarations in
the installed app. It also refuses one that reads a value or part the build doesn't
produce in that layout. The app then shows the last version it accepted, the bundled copy
or its fallback. It never shows a partial render.

These checks lower the chance of a broken update. They don't replace your own content
review or your reading of store policy. [Controlled delivery](/controlled-delivery)
covers rollback, freeze and the audit log.
