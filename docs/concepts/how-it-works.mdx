---
title: How it works
description: Follow a Restage surface from the widget you wrote, through the build and the device checks, to the screen.
---

A Restage surface starts as a Flutter widget you annotate. The build compiles it, the SDK
on the device fetches and checks the live version, and Flutter renders it inside your
app. Both the build and the SDK work from the same list of widgets your app has
installed, called the catalog.

## Write and compile

A surface is a plain Flutter widget with `@Screen()` or `@Paywall()`, or a flow with
`@FlowGraph(surface: ...)`. A screen published on its own declares its category with
`@Screen(surface: Surface.<category>)`, and a bare `@Screen()` is a step inside a flow.
Your own widgets join the catalog with `@RestageWidget`.

The categories are the `Surface` enum: `onboarding`, `message`, `survey`, `paywall`, and
`general`. `@Paywall()` implies `Surface.paywall`. `Surface.general` covers anything
else, whether a single screen or a whole flow.

`dart run build_runner build` runs `restage_codegen`. It reads your source with the Dart
analyzer, checks every widget and value against the catalog, and writes:

- `.rfw`, the binary file the app renders;
- `.rfwtxt`, a readable copy of it for review;
- a small file listing what the surface needs from the app, used for compatibility
  checks;
- for each standalone `@Screen(surface: ...)` or `@Paywall()`, a typed mount,
  `<ClassName>Surface`, that takes the same constructor parameters as your widget and shows your widget if the published
  version can't render;
- for flows, generated Dart and a `.flow.json` document;
- `.restage/build/metadata/restage.publication.json`, which records each surface's id and the
  files that make it up;
- optionally, an A2UI catalog in Dart and a standalone `.a2ui.json` document.

Anything the compiler can't express is a build error. It never drops a property or
swaps in a similar widget without telling you.

The generator finds annotated widgets anywhere in the package. Folders are for
organizing your project and don't set a category or an id. If you omit the id, the
generator uses the file name. Give an explicit id when it has to survive a file move.

## The catalog

The catalog describes the widgets, properties, and value types that a released build of
your app can render. The compiler accepts only source it can express with that catalog,
and the SDK registers the matching widgets, including factories generated for your own
widgets.

What ships is data the app renders. Your widgets carry the logic, as Dart compiled into
your app. A published version composes and configures them. New behavior still ships as
an app release.

## On the device

Generated `*Surface(...)` widgets are the recommended mounts. They declare their
placement automatically and keep the original Flutter screen, paywall or flow as a
fallback.
Bundled surfaces load from the app's assets and need no account or network. When the app
is connected to Restage Cloud or your own server, the SDK asks for the live version and
checks it before rendering it.

A render deadline limits the initial wait for fresh content to 300 ms by default.
The fetch continues after a fallback appears. Each kind of mount has its own fallback:

- A generated `<ClassName>Surface` or `RestageScreen<E>` checks the version's id,
  category, required widgets, and events against what the build generated. If a check
  fails, the generated mount shows the widget you wrote unless you pass an
  `unavailable` policy, and `RestageScreen` follows the policy you pass it.
- A generated flow mount checks the flow's declarations and can fall back to a
  version it accepted earlier, a bundle or the authored flow. The lower-level `RestageFlowGraph<R>` uses
  the available content and the `FlowUnavailablePolicy` your app supplies.
- `RestagePaywall` uses an accepted version saved on the device, a bundled copy,
  or its `fallbackBuilder`. Generated paywall mounts supply the original widget.

See [Hosted delivery](/delivery/hosted-delivery) for setup and the exact fallback order.

## Rendering and events

The SDK builds real Flutter widgets inside your widget tree. Theme reads resolve against
the current `ThemeData`, and [host data](/authoring/host-data) passed through a
generated mount's typed inputs resolves under `data.context.*`, rebuilding only the widgets that read
a changed value. Flutter still owns layout, accessibility, scrolling, and gestures.

Events come back to your app through `SurfaceEvent<T>` declarations and
`surfaceEvent(...)` in the widget, or through typed flow actions. A generated reference
only produces the events the class declares, so you never look events up by name. A flow
can call an action your app registered, with arguments that match its schema, and use
the result. The handler itself stays in your app.

By default, the SDK picks up a newly published version the next time a surface mounts.
[Live refresh](/delivery/live-refresh) can also update a surface already on screen when
the app resumes, when Restage Cloud or your own channel signals a change, or when you
call `Restage.reloadSurfaces()`.
