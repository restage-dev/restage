## What can a published update change?

A published version is a description that your app's widgets draw. It's written in a small, closed language that the compiler checks. It can change copy, layout, styling and flow routing. It can use simple logic, like conditions over data your app supplies. It can also choose which of your registered events and actions a control uses. For parts of a screen that render from your app, it can change where they go and what's in their slots.

Gestures, drawing, network calls and native work stay in Dart compiled into your app. New kinds of behavior still ship as an app release. That includes the behavior of a part that renders from your app, the values your app computes, the actions it provides and any new app-rendered part. The [App Store compliance](/app-store-compliance) page covers the boundary.

## Does local development need an account?

No. Authoring, code generation and generated mounts all work offline without surface assets. A generated mount falls back to your compiled widget. You need a backend only to publish versions to installed apps, measure them or run experiments.

## Where do custom drawing and app actions live?

In your app. Custom painting, gestures and animation live in your app code, wherever you wrote them. The build leaves them rendering from your app, and they need no annotation. Network calls and other app operations can be exposed to flows as typed host actions. Both ship in an app release. A surface then configures the widgets and calls the actions. [Authoring constraints](/authoring/constraints) lists what the compiler supports.

## What happens to code Restage can't deliver?

It renders from your app's compiled code. The build delivers what it can and leaves the rest where you wrote it. Run `restage build report` to see which parts those are. Run `restage widget promote` to move one into a widget class of your own. [What renders from your app](/authoring/islands) explains the split.

## Can a surface show data my app already has?

Yes. Declare it as constructor parameters on the screen, or pass a `context:` map to any mount. The surface reads it under `data.context.*`. A list parameter becomes a real loop, with the row count coming from the data. The values stay on the device. [Host data](/authoring/host-data) covers what's accepted and how updates rebuild.

## Can I serve surfaces myself?

Yes. Generated artifacts are files you can bundle with the app or serve from your own backend or CDN. The SDK works without Restage Cloud. Point `baseUrl` in `Restage.configure` at your server. Restage Cloud adds publishing, rollback, measurement and experiments.

## What happens when a fetch fails?

The app keeps showing a version it already accepted when it can. If it can't, it falls back to the bundled copy or your compiled widget. Generated screen and paywall mounts keep your widget, and generated flow mounts keep the authored flow. Lower-level mounts use the fallback policy your app supplies.

A malformed or incompatible version is rejected before it renders.

## Which widget mounts each kind of surface?

- A single screen mounts through its generated `<ClassName>Surface` widget, or through
  `RestageScreen<E>` with its generated reference.
- A flow mounts through its generated `<FlowName>Surface` widget. Use
  `RestageFlowGraph<R>` when supplying the reference and fallback yourself. A
  general flow can return a `Map<String, Object?>`.
- A paywall mounts through its generated `<ClassName>Surface` widget. It falls back to your compiled widget. You can also mount it directly through `RestagePaywall` with your own fallback setup.

Every generated mount has an implicit [placement](/delivery/placements). Use its ordinary constructor, and `.atPlacement('name', ...)` if you need to override the name. `RestagePlacement` is the lower-level API for manual setup.

Messages and surveys run on the same flow runtime as onboarding. They need no separate widget.

## Which surface categories are supported?

`Surface` is an enum with five values: `onboarding`, `message`, `survey`, `paywall` and `general`. Use `Surface.general` for anything that fits none of the other four, whether it's a single screen or a whole flow. There are no free-form category strings.

## Can a paywall appear inside another flow?

Yes. An onboarding, message, survey or general flow can move to a `@Paywall()` screen and finish on one of its events. The flow keeps its category.

## Where does a surface's id come from?

From the annotation. If you leave the id out, the generator uses the file name. Give an explicit id if it has to survive a file move. The folder, the output path and the CLI's `--type` flag don't affect it.

## What data does Restage collect?

With no backend configured, none. Generated mounts show your compiled widgets (or optional bundled content) and call no server.

Once the app is connected, the SDK measures each published version by default. It captures views, taps and completions automatically, with no tracking calls to add. Pass `measurementEnabled: false` to `Restage.configure` to switch measurement off. [Telemetry and data](/trust/telemetry) lists what's sent and every control.

## What is open source?

The SDK, catalogs, schemas, CLI, MCP server, A2UI check and examples are BSD-3-Clause. The two build-time compiler packages use FSL-1.1-ALv2. Restage Cloud is proprietary. [Licensing](/trust/licensing) has the full package list.

## What does it cost?

The SDK and the build-time packages are free to use under their licenses. Restage Cloud has a free plan and paid plans. See [restage.dev/pricing](https://restage.dev/pricing) for current plans.
