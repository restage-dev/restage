## What can a published update change?

What ships is data the app renders. Your widgets carry the logic, as Dart compiled into
your app. A published version composes and configures them: copy, layout, styling, flow
routing, and which of your registered events and actions a control uses. New behavior
still ships as an app release. The [App Store compliance](/app-store-compliance) page
covers the boundary.

## Does local development need an account?

No. Authoring, code generation and generated mounts with compiled-widget fallbacks
work offline without surface assets. You
need a backend only to publish versions to installed apps, measure them, or run
experiments.

## Where do custom drawing and app actions live?

Custom painting, gestures, and animation live in your own widgets, registered with
`@RestageWidget`. Network calls and other app operations can be exposed to flows as
typed host actions. Both ship in an app release; a surface then configures the widgets
and calls the actions. [Authoring constraints](/authoring/constraints) lists what the
compiler supports.

## Can a surface show data my app already has?

Yes. Declare it as constructor parameters on the screen, or pass a `context:` map to any
mount, and the surface reads it under `data.context.*`. A list parameter becomes a real
loop, so the row count comes from the data. The values stay on the device.
[Host data](/authoring/host-data) covers what's accepted and how updates rebuild.

## Can I serve surfaces myself?

Yes. Generated artifacts are files you can bundle with the app or serve from your own
backend or CDN, and the SDK works without Restage Cloud. Point `baseUrl` in
`Restage.configure` at your server. Restage Cloud adds publishing, rollback,
measurement, and experiments on top.

## What happens when a fetch fails?

The app keeps showing a version it already accepted when it can, and otherwise falls
back to the bundled copy or compiled original. Generated screen and paywall mounts
retain your widget; generated flow mounts retain the authored flow. Lower-level
mounts use the fallback policy your app supplies. A malformed or incompatible version is rejected before it renders.

## Which widget mounts each kind of surface?

- A single screen mounts through its generated `<ClassName>Surface` widget, or through
  `RestageScreen<E>` with its generated reference.
- A flow mounts through its generated `<FlowName>Surface` widget. Use
  `RestageFlowGraph<R>` when supplying the reference and fallback yourself. A
  general flow can return a `Map<String, Object?>`.
- A paywall mounts through its generated `<ClassName>Surface` widget with a compiled
  fallback, or directly through `RestagePaywall` with your own fallback setup.
Every generated mount has an implicit [placement](/delivery/placements). Use its
ordinary constructor. `.atPlacement('name', ...)` optionally overrides the name;
`RestagePlacement` is the lower-level API for manual setup.

Messages and surveys run on the same flow runtime as onboarding and need no separate
widget.

## Which surface categories are supported?

`Surface` is an enum with five values: `onboarding`, `message`, `survey`, `paywall`, and
`general`. `Surface.general` is for anything that doesn't fit the other four, whether a
single screen or a whole flow. There are no free-form category strings.

## Can a paywall appear inside another flow?

Yes. An onboarding, message, survey, or general flow can move to a `@Paywall()` screen
and finish on one of its events. Adding a paywall step doesn't change the flow's
category.

## Where does a surface's id come from?

From the annotation. If you omit the id, the generator uses the file name. Give an
explicit id when it needs to survive a file move. The folder, the output path, and the
CLI's `--type` flag don't affect it.

## What data does Restage collect?

With no backend configured, none: generated mounts use their compiled fallbacks
(or optional bundled content) and call no server.
Once the app is connected, the SDK measures each published version by default. Views,
taps, and completions are captured automatically, with no tracking calls to add. Pass
`measurementEnabled: false` to `Restage.configure` to switch measurement off.
[Telemetry and data](/trust/telemetry) lists what is sent and every control.

## What is open source?

The SDK, catalogs, schemas, CLI, MCP server, A2UI check, and examples are BSD-3-Clause.
The two build-time compiler packages use FSL-1.1-ALv2. Restage Cloud is proprietary.
[Licensing](/trust/licensing) has the complete package split.

## What does it cost?

The SDK and the build-time packages are free to use under their licenses. Restage Cloud
has a free plan and paid plans. See
[restage.dev/pricing](https://restage.dev/pricing) for current plans.
