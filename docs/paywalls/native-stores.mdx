`restage_store_bridge` connects paywall buttons to Flutter's `in_app_purchase` plugin. It handles store updates and finishing transactions. Your app verifies each transaction and delivers the entitlement or consumable content.

<Callout type="info">
The package is only available from source. It isn't on pub.dev yet.
</Callout>

The [runnable example](https://github.com/restage-dev/restage/tree/main/packages/restage_store_bridge/example) has a paywall, its generated mount, loading and error states, and an alternative setup for an existing purchase service.

## Adapter helpers

| API | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `InAppPurchaseScope` | Owns one controller above navigation. Disposes it and recovers Android purchases at startup and resume. |
| `InAppPurchaseController` | Runs native purchase updates through your verification and delivery callbacks. |
| `inAppPurchaseEventHandler` | Connects paywall purchase and restore events to that controller. |
| `inAppPurchaseCustomEventHandler` | Connects a purchase service your app already owns. |
| `inAppPurchaseOfferingData` | Builds the display context for a direct `RestagePaywall` mount. |

Use the scope when the bridge owns the purchase stream. If your app already owns that stream, use the [custom service path](#purchase-attribution) instead of starting a second controller. `StorePaywallSurface` is generated from your own widget. The adapter doesn't export a prebuilt paywall.

## Keep one controller above navigation

Configure [hosted delivery](/delivery/hosted-delivery), then wrap your app in `InAppPurchaseScope`. `billing` and `reportError` are your app's verification, delivery and error handlers:

```dart
import 'package:flutter/widgets.dart';
import 'package:restage_store_bridge/restage_store_bridge.dart';

// Inside main(), after Flutter initialization and Restage configuration:
runApp(
  InAppPurchaseScope(
    create: () => InAppPurchaseController(
      verifyPurchase: billing.verifyPurchase,
      deliverPurchase: billing.deliverPurchase,
      consumableProductIds: const {'coins'},
      onError: reportError,
    ),
    child: const MyApp(),
  ),
);
```

The scope creates the controller once, refreshes Android purchases at startup and resume, and disposes the controller when the scope is removed. Keep it above `MaterialApp` and navigation, and closing a paywall won't interrupt a transaction. Apple restore stays user-initiated.

## Supply products and mount the paywall

Query the store with `InAppPurchase.instance.queryProductDetails`. Handle its errors and any missing products before you show purchase buttons. Then build an immutable `Map<String, ProductDetails>` whose keys name the offers your paywall shows. On Google Play, pick the base plan and offer you want and keep those exact `ProductDetails` objects.

Use the same map to display prices and to dispatch purchases. Mount the example's generated `StorePaywallSurface` from a descendant of the scope:

```dart
StorePaywallSurface(
  packages: [
    for (final entry in packages.entries)
      PaywallPackage(
        identifier: entry.key,
        priceString: entry.value.price,
        billingTerms: billingTerms[entry.key]!,
      ),
  ],
  onEvent: inAppPurchaseEventHandler(
    packages: packages,
    controller: InAppPurchaseScope.of(context),
    onError: reportError,
  ),
)
```

`PaywallPackage` and `StorePaywallSurface` come from the [example](https://github.com/restage-dev/restage/blob/main/packages/restage_store_bridge/example/lib/store_upgrade.dart). `billingTerms` is your app's accurate, localized trial, billing-period and renewal text for each offer. A catalog price can describe an introductory or free phase. It doesn't prove what the user paid or whether they're eligible.

The example widget sends `in_app_purchase.purchase` with a `packageIdentifier` argument that matches the map key. Restore sends `in_app_purchase.restore`. An unknown or malformed key produces an error. The generated mount sends the typed display values and keeps your compiled widget as the fallback.

For a direct `RestagePaywall` mount, `inAppPurchaseOfferingData` gives you identifiers, localized prices and currencies as host data. It doesn't include billing terms. Direct mounts also need widget registration and a fallback.

## Verify, deliver, then finish

`verifyPurchase` takes a `PurchaseDetails` update and returns `Future<bool>`. Check it with your trusted purchase validation service. If it returns false, the purchase isn't delivered or finished.

`deliverPurchase` returns `Future<void>`. Record each delivery by its verified transaction id, and a purchase the store sends again can't grant content twice.

The controller verifies, delivers, and then completes or consumes the transaction. Errors go to `onError`. `retryPendingPurchases()` retries the stage that failed and skips stages already completed in memory.

The bridge has no persistent retry queue. After a restart, recovery depends on the store sending the purchase again, Android's owned-purchase refresh, and your app's saved delivery record.

List the consumable product IDs you support, including ones that can arrive through recovery. The default controller handles single-product Google transactions. Other transaction shapes need your own purchase service.

## Purchase attribution

The default controller doesn't report paywall conversions on its own. When the paywall starts a native purchase, it can't know which transaction will arrive later. The purchase stream also carries restored, recovered and external purchases. Matching on the product id or the timing isn't enough.

If your purchase service can reliably tie a new transaction to the checkout that started it, use `inAppPurchaseCustomEventHandler`. Its purchase callback returns that checkout's verified and delivered `PurchaseDetails`, or null if no new purchase was confirmed. The handler reports the confirmed purchase using the original paywall event.

Your service finishes the native transaction, drops duplicates, and links each purchase to its checkout. Restore never reports a conversion.

See the [custom service example](https://github.com/restage-dev/restage/blob/main/packages/restage_store_bridge/example/lib/custom.dart) and [purchase reporting](/paywalls/products-and-purchases#measure-completed-purchases). If your app uses RevenueCat, use the [RevenueCat adapter](/paywalls/revenuecat).
