---
title: Products and purchases
description: Show live store prices and connect paywall buttons to purchases in your app.
---

A paywall displays the products your app supplies and sends an event when a user
chooses one. Your app handles the event through its store integration. Prices,
offers and entitlement checks stay with that integration.

Pass product identifiers and localized prices as ordinary widget constructor
parameters. The generated mount supplies them as
[host data](/authoring/host-data); the SDK does not populate a `data.products`
namespace or fetch prices for the widget.

## Choose an integration

Use `restage_revenuecat` if your app already uses RevenueCat. For Flutter's
`in_app_purchase`, use `restage_store_bridge` with its scope and controller, or
connect an existing purchase service through its custom handler. Both work with
your authored paywall's generated mount. Neither replaces your entitlement checks.

## Use RevenueCat packages

For an app that already uses RevenueCat, use `restage_revenuecat` to pass an offering
into the paywall and handle its buttons:

```dart
RevenueCatPaywallSurface(
  packages: [
    for (final package in offering.availablePackages)
      PaywallPackage(
        identifier: package.identifier,
        priceString: package.storeProduct.priceString,
      ),
  ],
  onEvent: revenueCatEventHandler(
    offering: offering,
    onCustomerInfo: updateFromRevenueCat,
    onError: handlePurchaseError,
  ),
)
```

`RevenueCatPaywallSurface` and `PaywallPackage` come from the
[adapter example](https://github.com/restage-dev/restage/tree/main/packages/restage_revenuecat/example).
`offering` is the RevenueCat offering the app fetched. `updateFromRevenueCat` and
`handlePurchaseError` are your app’s callbacks. The offering supplies each package’s
identifier, localized price and available trial or introductory offer. Display the
package’s `priceString` and send its identifier from the same button:

```dart
FilledButton(
  onPressed: paywallEvent(
    'revenuecat.purchase',
    args: {'packageIdentifier': package.identifier},
  ),
  child: Text(package.priceString),
)
```

The adapter resolves that identifier within the supplied offering and purchases the
RevenueCat package. An unknown identifier produces an error. A restore button sends
`revenuecat.restore`.

After this integration is in the app, you can add packages to the offering and
publish paywall versions selecting them without changing an app-side mapping.
The [RevenueCat guide](/paywalls/revenuecat) includes worked examples for a price
test and holiday pricing, including rollback.

## Use the native stores

For Flutter's `in_app_purchase`, `restage_store_bridge` handles paywall events,
store updates, and transaction completion. Your app verifies each purchase on a
trusted server and delivers the entitlement or content exactly once. See [Native stores](/paywalls/native-stores).

## Use another store integration

Pass the values your app already knows through [host data](/authoring/host-data).
The paywall can receive a list of products with identifiers and localized prices,
then send the selected identifier to your `onEvent` callback. Validate it against
the supplied products before purchasing.

Use event names belonging to your integration. `purchase`, `restore`, and the
`restage.` purchase and restore names are reserved. Keep subscription and entitlement
checks in your app; rendering a paywall or receiving a button event grants nothing.

## Measure completed purchases

The RevenueCat adapter reports a conversion after a successful purchase, linked to
the paywall view that led to it. Choose **Purchase conversion** when creating the
experiment. The result represents accepted app-reported purchases. Restores,
failures and cancellations are excluded.

For another integration, report only a new, verified and delivered purchase that
your purchase service can tie to the checkout the paywall started. Use that
checkout's `RestageEvent` and the purchased identifiers:

```dart
await Restage.measurement.reportPurchase(
  event,
  packageIdentifier: selectedPackageIdentifier,
  productIdentifier: selectedProductIdentifier,
);
```

A matching product id or a nearby purchase-stream update is not enough to establish
that connection. Restores and recovered transactions must not become new conversions.
The default native store controller therefore does not report conversions
automatically; the [custom service path](/paywalls/native-stores#purchase-attribution)
needs your app to link each purchase to its checkout.

The event stays linked to the paywall view it came from through a refresh or dismissal.
Each event sends at most one report. The method returns `false` if reporting is
unavailable or refused, including after `Restage.reset()`.

A tap metric remains useful for measuring button activation. Without a purchase
report, it cannot tell you whether checkout completed. RevenueCat’s Flutter
purchase result carries no paid amount or currency; the adapter leaves those values
unknown and keeps catalog prices as display data.

See [Entitlements](/paywalls/entitlements) for entitlement checks and restore.
