---
title: Why Restage
description: Author surfaces in the Flutter you already write, publish them without an app release, and keep your app in charge of behavior.
---

Restage starts from the Flutter code and design system you already have. Add an
annotation to a widget, and it becomes a surface you can publish, measure, and roll back
without an app release: an onboarding flow, a message, a survey, a paywall, or any
screen.

## Start with Flutter

You write a surface with Flutter widgets, in the same repository as the rest of the app.
`restage_codegen` follows the annotated widget tree, checks it against the widgets your
app has installed, and writes a [Remote Flutter Widgets](https://pub.dev/packages/rfw)
artifact. Theme reads, structured values such as `TextStyle` and `BoxDecoration`,
interactive state, and your own components carry through.

Mark a component with `@RestageWidget` to add it to the catalog. A component built only
from catalog widgets can be inlined into the artifact. A component with its own logic is
registered with a generated factory and rendered by the code already in your app. Its
properties can be plain data classes and lists of structured values.

The compiler handles a bounded part of Flutter. When it can't represent something, the
build stops and points at the line. It doesn't guess.
[Authoring constraints](/authoring/constraints) lists the limits.

## Your app owns the behavior

What ships is data the app renders. Your widgets carry the logic, as Dart compiled into
your app. A published version composes and configures them. New behavior still ships as
an app release.

A surface can arrange catalog widgets, fire the events and actions your app registered,
and set a flow's data and routing. Widget implementations, network access, and action
handlers come from your app build.

That boundary does more than help with app review. It gives the compiler clear
rules to check against, keeps every version readable, and lets a rollback restore an
earlier published version exactly. The [App Store compliance](/app-store-compliance)
page covers it in more detail.

## Theme values resolve on the device

A surface that reads `Theme.of(context)` resolves it inside the running widget tree. It
follows the app into dark mode and picks up a later design-system change without a
rebuild. A surface can also use fixed colors where the design calls for them.

## Every version is measured

Analytics and A/B tests are in beta. Once the app is connected to Restage Cloud, the SDK measures each published version.
Views, taps, and completions are captured automatically, with no tracking calls to add.
Measurement is on by default; pass `measurementEnabled: false` to `Restage.configure` to
switch it off. [Telemetry and data](/trust/telemetry) lists what is recorded.

## Failures have a defined outcome

Source the compiler can't handle stops the build. On the device, a fetched version is
checked against the installed app before it replaces what is on screen. If a check or a
fetch fails:

- a generated screen mount shows the widget you wrote;
- a paywall uses its last accepted version, a bundle, or its compiled fallback;
- a flow uses its last accepted version, its bundled copy, or the
  `FlowUnavailablePolicy` your app chose.

[Live refresh](/delivery/live-refresh) of a surface already on screen goes through the
same checks.

## One runtime for every surface

Onboarding, messages, surveys, and paywalls all use the same compiler and runtime.
Use generated `*Surface(...)` widgets to mount screens, paywalls and flows. They
supply typed inputs and events, a compiled fallback and an automatic placement. A flow with
general delivery returns a `Map<String, Object?>`. Paywall purchase events go to
your app, which uses its existing store library or an adapter.

Direct `RestageScreen`, `RestagePaywall` and `RestageFlowGraph` mounts remain
available for apps that supply their own references and fallback setup.

The same runtime works for a smaller region too, such as a card inside an existing
`Scaffold`. Your app decides how much of a screen is delivered.

## You keep the artifacts

Generated `.rfw` and flow files can be bundled, committed, inspected, and served from
your own server or CDN. The SDK works without Restage Cloud. The runtime and everything
that ships in your app are BSD-3-Clause, the build-time compiler is FSL-1.1-ALv2, and
Restage Cloud is a proprietary, optional service. See [Licensing](/trust/licensing) for
the package-by-package split.
